# Full audit — August 2026

Nine specialist agents audited the extension after the trim that removed roughly a third of
its surface. This is the complete record: what was found, what was fixed, and what was
deliberately not.

**Why it was worth doing:** the suite was green at 413 tests and ~95% coverage before this,
and the audit still found a control that keyboard users could not reach at all. A green suite
proves nothing was broken by a change; it says nothing about what was never covered.

Two findings were reported independently by two agents each — the models-bridge reply gap
(security + typescript) and the tab keyboard trap (a11y + tests). Agreement across separate
remits is the strongest signal in a report like this.

### Where the audit's recommendation was not followed

**The base URL finding was a different bug than reported.** The audit read openai's `baseUrl`
passthrough as deliberate proxy support and asked for an advisory warning in the options UI.
Checking the form first: it renders that field only when the descriptor says `needsBaseUrl`,
and `handleProviderChange` blanks the value when you switch to a provider that does not — so
no sequence of clicks produces an openai base URL, and a warning would have had no audience.
What the passthrough actually left open was a route for a hand-edited or corrupted stored
value to redirect a request carrying the user's key to a host they never chose. Closed at the
factory. `custom` remains the supported way to point at a proxy, and it demands the URL
explicitly. The test that asserted the passthrough now asserts its absence, with the reason.

**The quote narrowing was implemented, then reverted.** Requiring an echoed delimiter as
corroboration broke three legitimate cases immediately — quotes with no tag is the common
shape, not the exception. The asymmetry settles it: stray quotes left in are visible and a
user deletes them; quotes wrongly removed silently change what the text asserts, and that
needs an unquoted source _and_ a fully-quoted result containing no other quotation. The
residual risk is now argued in `src/prompts/sanitize.ts` rather than implied.

**`--border-strong` failed in both themes, not just light.** The audit measured 2.00:1 against
the light surface. Against `--surface-raised` — the tighter of the two surfaces these buttons
sit on — dark was 2.05:1 and light 2.00:1. Both darkened past 3:1, and `contrast.test.ts` now
carries the non-text threshold, so the rule is enforced on every future palette edit rather
than checked once by hand.

## Rejected

- **`refactor-cleaner` found no dead code.** Verified: every CSS class, export, dependency and
  test helper it checked is referenced. The trim cleaned up after itself.
- **Renaming the `copied-dirty` outcome.** The name is awkward, its comment disambiguates it,
  and a rename costs a literal plus its consumer for no behavioural gain. Opportunistic at best.
- **Splitting `ReplaceOutcome` into an outcome plus a `pageDirty` flag.** Only one variant needs
  the extra bit; a flag re-introduces the "which combinations are valid" question that the flat
  union exists to avoid.

## What the imports were worth

`a11y-architect` and `wcag-audit-patterns` earned their place — three CRITICAL findings and all
the contrast arithmetic came from them, in an area `known-issues.md` had already admitted was
unexamined.

`performance-optimizer` was imported and **not run**. The first two waves filled the available
budget, and its remit — content-script weight, render behaviour during streaming — had no
reported symptom behind it. It stays in `.claude/agents/` for a pass that has one.

## How the audit was run, for next time

The first attempt launched eleven agents at once, each told to audit the extension, and
exhausted the session limit — three burned about 100k tokens apiece before dying. What worked
was scoping each agent to five or so named files, matching the model to the work (opus for the
adversarial and async reasoning, sonnet for the criteria-driven passes, haiku for the
grep-and-verify sweeps), and running in waves of three. The same nine agents then averaged
under fifteen tool calls each.
